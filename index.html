Legend · what we capture

Three kinds of observation

CLASS P

Places

A place is a fixed point with a name, an entrance and an address as it is seen on the ground.

  • Entrance position and access notes
  • Address as posted, with the source photo
  • Open or closed at the time of the visit
CLASS S

Storefronts

A storefront record describes what a shop shows to the street: its trade, its signs and its posted hours.

  • Trade category from the sign and the stock in view
  • Posted hours and payment signs
  • Shopfront photo, with faces and plates blurred
CLASS C

Conditions

A condition is a state of a place at a time: a road surface, a flood line, a queue, a power cut.

  • Condition type from a fixed list
  • Observed-at time and an operator’s confidence
  • Repeat visits join the same place by reference

Route · from plan to listing

How an observation becomes a listing

  1. Plan the area

    A campaign names the grid cells, the observation classes and the refresh interval. An agent on CommandAGI drafts the route and checks it against the capture standard before any operator leaves.

  2. Capture in the field

    An operator visits with a phone. The record holds the photo, the position, the time, the method note and the operator’s confidence. Nothing is filled in later from memory.

  3. Normalize the record

    An agent and Fieldlight’s normalization lead map each record to the standard, keep the raw record beside the clean one, and reject what does not fit with a stated reason.

  4. Publish the stream

    The result is a data-stream listing on the CommandAGI marketplace, with its schema, coverage map and update cadence. Subscribers receive new records as they are accepted.

Note · ground truth
